# Identity

## Name and brand architecture

**Uncle Buck's Laboratory** is the master brand. Possessive, with the apostrophe: the lab belongs to Uncle Buck. Handles and domains drop the apostrophe: `unclebuckslab` everywhere, `unclebuckslab.com` for the site. The GitHub organization is `unclebuckslab`.

Each project keeps its own name and carries an endorsement line: *Congress Cards, from Uncle Buck's Laboratory.* While the lab has no audience, every project builds attention for the one name. A project earns a brand of its own only after it has an audience of its own.

"AI," "agentic" and "research" stay out of the name. They go in the tagline, which can change.

## The persona

Uncle Buck is the founder's public persona: openly the founder, not a secret identity. Everything the lab publishes comes from the desk of Uncle Buck. The AI agents are his lab rats, the squad that does the digging.

The persona can joke. The work can't. Numbers, sources and claims stay plain and checkable, whatever the tone around them.

On money, Uncle Buck is loudly ethical and openly buyable by brands he already likes, and he says so. The money rules are what make that safe to say. See [MONEY_RULES.md](MONEY_RULES.md).

The full character guide (`CHARACTER.md`) and voice guide (`VOICE.md`) come out of milestone B1.

## Mission

Explore whatever is interesting. Build things that are useful or informative to other people. Find out how far an agent-supported company can go, and do it in the open.

Show the benefits of AI and be just as plain about its costs, its failures and the responsibility to roll it out in a way that protects the economy, people's freedoms and the planet. Innovation should make the world better for everyone, not a select few.

## What the lab makes

| Type | Example | Public form | Playbook |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recurring report | AI Money Report | Issue archive plus the lab email list | [report.md](../playbooks/report.md) |
| Dashboard or live data product | Denver Neighborhood Sales | A live page on a refresh schedule | [data-product.md](../playbooks/data-product.md) |
| Reference database | Congress Cards | One sourced page per entity | data-product plus the trust rules |
| Visual, map or data deck | A chart or deck explaining one finding | Embedded on a project page | Written when the first one starts |
| Brand or content channel | A music prediction brand (parked) | Social account plus a page on the lab site | Written when the first one starts |
| Product or tool | Something built to solve one problem | An app or page, possibly paid | Written when the first one starts |
| Writing | Field Notes, then a weekly blog | Posts on the lab site | Written when the first one starts |

Every project, whatever its type, has a registry record, a page on the lab site, a `CLAUDE.md`, a decision log, a lab notebook and a stage.

## Lifecycle

Idea → Explore → Define → Build → Review → Launch → Operate → Improve or Archive. Not every project uses every stage.

Status labels (**In the lab**, **Live**, **Paused**, **Archived**) are internal: the registry and Lab Desk use them. The site shows only live work, with no status or stage labels; work in progress is covered in the lab dispatch. Whether archived projects stay on the site is undecided.
