# Trust rules

These rules apply to every publication, chart, post and AI output from the lab. A draft that breaks one doesn't ship. They started in the AI Money Report and are now lab-wide.

## Sourcing

1. **No source, no claim.** Every number links to a primary document: a filing, a law, a dataset.
2. **A harm claim needs a document.** A layoff is logged as a layoff unless a filing ties it to AI.
3. **State the gaps.** Coverage limits appear next to the data they affect.
4. **Our own beliefs get checked hardest.** A claim we expect to be true is verified like any other.

## Labels

Every claim about why something happened carries one of three labels:

- **Fact:** what the document shows.
- **Stated:** what the company or official says, in their own filings or remarks.
- **Our read:** our analysis, clearly marked.

## Criticism

5. **Criticize documented conduct, not character.** Pay, stock sales, spending, votes, enforcement actions.
6. **A lawsuit filed is not a finding.** Write "accused of" until a court or regulator rules.
7. **No motives without documents.** "They spent X" is fine. "They spent X to crush Y" needs proof.
8. **One standard for everyone:** companies we like, companies the founder might work with, every party and every member of Congress.
9. **Opinion lives in labelled opinion.** Reporting sections stay factual.

## Points of view

10. A project may hold a declared point of view; it says so on its page. The AI Money Report argues for AI regulation and accountability, and addresses the strongest counterarguments directly. Congress Cards holds no point of view on parties or members.
11. Every claim about a law cites the law itself.

## Reader protection

12. Financial content says it isn't investment advice. No trading on a story before it publishes (see [MONEY_RULES.md](MONEY_RULES.md)).
13. AI use is disclosed: what the agents did, and that a human reviewed it.
14. Mistakes are corrected publicly, in a corrections log on the project's page.
